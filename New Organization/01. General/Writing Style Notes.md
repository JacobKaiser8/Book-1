### **Core Traits of Third-Person Limited (Adult Fantasy Style)**

1. **Deep Interior Access**  
    You narrate _what the character sees, thinks, remembers, or feels_, but not what others are thinking unless the POV character observes it.
2. **Immersive but Controlled Description**  
    Describe the world through the _lens of the POV character's biases, fears, goals,_ and _background knowledge_. Let description reflect emotion.
3. **Tone Matches Character**  
    Each scene “sounds” like the character. A soldier sees terrain tactically. A scholar notices inscriptions. A slave focuses on escape paths or hunger.
4. **No Head-Hopping**  
    Stay with one POV at a time. If you want to switch POVs, start a new scene or chapter.
5. **Language: Mature, Not Flowery**  
    You can use rich vocabulary, but aim for clean, sharp prose—grounded in action, thought, and feeling rather than ornate style.

### 🧱 Tips to Nail the Style in Your Book

|Technique|Description|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**Sensory Filtering**|Only include what the POV character would notice.|"The stench of burning fat clung to the wind—a scent Marcus couldn’t unlearn."|
|**Emotional Echoing**|Let emotion bleed into neutral descriptions.|"The tower stood quiet. Too quiet. Like it knew he was coming."|
|**Controlled Action**|Don't over-explain combat or magic. Ground it in intent and stakes.|"He let the fire slip to his hands. Not too fast. Not yet. Let them think he was unarmed."|
|**Gaps in Knowledge**|The narrator doesn’t know what the character doesn’t know.|"The man smiled. Marcus hated smiles like that—too sure, too calm. He didn't know why, but it made him grip the hilt tighter."|
