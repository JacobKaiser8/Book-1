### **Core Traits of Third-Person Limited (Adult Fantasy Style)**

1. **Deep Interior Access**  
    You narrate _what the character sees, thinks, remembers, or feels_, but not what others are thinking unless the POV character observes it.
2. **Immersive but Controlled Description**  
    Describe the world through the _lens of the POV character's biases, fears, goals,_ and _background knowledge_. Let description reflect emotion.
3. **Tone Matches Character**  
    Each scene “sounds” like the character. A soldier sees terrain tactically. A scholar notices inscriptions. A slave focuses on escape paths or hunger.
4. **No Head-Hopping**  
    Stay with one POV at a time. If you want to switch POVs, start a new scene or chapter.
5. **Language: Mature, Not Flowery**  
    You can use rich vocabulary, but aim for clean, sharp prose—grounded in action, thought, and feeling rather than ornate style.

### 🧱 Tips to Nail the Style in Your Book

|Technique|Description|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**Sensory Filtering**|Only include what the POV character would notice.|"The stench of burning fat clung to the wind—a scent Marcus couldn’t unlearn."|
|**Emotional Echoing**|Let emotion bleed into neutral descriptions.|"The tower stood quiet. Too quiet. Like it knew he was coming."|
|**Controlled Action**|Don't over-explain combat or magic. Ground it in intent and stakes.|"He let the fire slip to his hands. Not too fast. Not yet. Let them think he was unarmed."|
|**Gaps in Knowledge**|The narrator doesn’t know what the character doesn’t know.|"The man smiled. Marcus hated smiles like that—too sure, too calm. He didn't know why, but it made him grip the hilt tighter."|

# Marcus

When Marcus is the main character, you’ll want to **immerse the reader in his head** — not by telling them what he feels, but by letting them experience the world _as he experiences it_.  
That means **third-person limited** with his personality, worldview, and emotional state subtly influencing **every description, observation, and choice of words**.

Here’s how to approach it for Marcus specifically:

---

## 1️⃣ **Anchor the POV in Marcus’s Mind**

- Only describe **what Marcus notices** — the details he _cares about_ or _is trained to see_.
- In early acts, he might notice **form, posture, and weapons** (military training), but overlook political nuance or subtle emotional cues.
- By Act III and IV, his focus may shift — noticing **danger, suffering, and tells of deceit** rather than the shine of armor or order of ranks.

**Example — Act I Marcus:**

> The Iskari came over the ridge in a loose, sloppy advance. Marcus counted three with shields, the rest unarmored. They didn’t even hold formation.  
> (_Shows his early confidence — assessing them by training standards._)

**Example — Act III Marcus:**

> The Iskari came over the ridge in a loose advance. No armor, no formation — just hungry eyes and blades already wet. They weren’t here to win a battle. They were here to feed the fire.  
> (_Now he reads intent, not just form — darker, more experienced lens._)

---

## 2️⃣ **Filter Through His Emotional State**

- Marcus’s **inner temperature** should color the narration.
- In Act I, his world is brighter, cleaner — metaphors may draw on ideals of glory or order.
- In later acts, language becomes heavier, more tactile, sometimes bitter.

**Example — Act I:**

> The banners caught the wind, snapping proud against the mountain air. Marcus straightened without thinking.

**Example — Act III:**

> The banners whipped against the wind, frayed and heavy with rain. Marcus didn’t look up.

---

## 3️⃣ **Show His Thoughts Through Action**

- Let his decisions, hesitations, and mistakes show how he’s feeling — avoid over-explaining.
- If he’s nervous, show it in the way he checks his grip, shifts weight, or avoids eye contact.

**Instead of:**

> Marcus felt unprepared.

**Use:**

> His sword felt heavier than it had in training. Too heavy.

---

## 4️⃣ **Use His Military Lens**

Marcus is trained — even when untested — so he’ll:

- Count opponents.
- Notice armor, weapons, and formations.
- Clock terrain for advantage or danger.
- Judge others’ discipline (or lack of it).

This keeps his perspective **consistent** and believable.

---

## 5️⃣ **Let His Relationship with Luphera Shape Tone**

- Before he knows her: fire is just a weapon or danger.
- After meeting her: fire imagery may take on **warmth, guidance, or shared will**.
- In Act III: fire may mirror **pain and resistance**.
- In Act IV: fire becomes **acceptance, sacrifice, and legacy**.

---

## 6️⃣ **Control the Scope**

- **Stay in his head**: we only know what Marcus sees, hears, or is told.
- If you need to hint at a bigger picture, do it through _what he misreads, questions, or suspects_.

---

## 7️⃣ **Match Pacing to His State of Mind**

- In battle: short, clipped sentences, quick observations, physical sensations.
- In reflection: longer, heavier sentences with more sensory detail.
- In moments of trauma or burnout: sensory overload, fragmented impressions.

---

1. **Only show what Marcus would notice.**  
    No omniscient detail — if he doesn’t see it, hear it, or learn it, the reader doesn’t either.
    
2. **Filter everything through his military upbringing and experiences.**  
    He notices weapons, armor, formations, and posture before colors or fashion.
    
3. **Evolving worldview = evolving language.**  
    Tone, metaphor, and pacing change as Marcus changes.
    
4. **Emotions are shown through sensory detail, not stated outright.**  
    If he’s afraid, show it in his breathing, grip, or what he fixates on.
    
5. **Fire imagery changes over time.**
    - Act I: fire = glory, strength.
    - Act II: fire = control, purpose.
    - Act III: fire = pain, defiance.
    - Act IV: fire = sacrifice, legacy.

## 🔥 **Writing Cues to Stay in Marcus’s POV**

- **Act I:** Pride in formation, youth’s certainty, notices symbols of glory.
- **Act II:** Tactical awareness, controlled aggression, weighing choices.
- **Act III:** Bitterness, compassion for innocents, anger at wasted lives.
- **Act IV:** Acceptance, clarity, self-sacrifice without hesitation.
## 🎯 **Marcus Veridion – Act I Voice Bank**

### **1. Observations & Description**

_Use these to show how Marcus sees the world at 18, still in the academy mindset._

- “The formation held, every step in time with the beat of the march.”
- “Banners caught the wind, proud against the blue of the sky.”
- “He counted the shields first, then the gaps between them.”
- “Polished helms and straight lines — the signs of a force worth following.”
- “The mountain air was sharp, but it carried the scent of home.”
- “Every spear was angled just so, every shield where it belonged.”

---

### **2. Physical Sensations**

_These bring the reader into his body — confident but keyed-up before his first real fight._

- “Sweat prickled under his collar, but it was the good kind — the kind before action.”
- “His grip on the hilt was firm, the leather warm from his palm.”
- “His heartbeat drummed in rhythm with the march.”
- “The armor weighed on his shoulders, a familiar pressure he wore like a promise.”
- “His boots bit into the hard-packed road with every step.”

---

### **3. Internal Drive & Confidence**

_These show his motivation without sounding arrogant — eager to prove himself._

- “Every drill had led here.”
- “This was no exercise — this was the moment he’d been waiting for.”
- “If he fought well today, they’d remember it.”
- “Every order, every correction — they would all be worth it now.”
- “The academy taught him to hold the line. Today, he’d see what that meant.”

---

### **4. Early Naivety**

_Subtle overconfidence — still romanticizing battle._

- “The enemy didn’t even hold formation. They’d fall quickly.”
- “He’d trained for worse than this.”
- “Discipline would win this fight before steel did.”
- “This was what honor looked like — meeting the enemy head-on.”
- “He could almost picture the story they’d tell after this.”

---

### **5. Action in Battle**

_Act I Marcus still moves like he’s in training — calculated, but without true battlefield chaos experience._

- “Step forward. Guard high. Drive the point.”
- “He moved as the instructors taught: cut, step in, push.”
- “The shield struck first, the sword followed close behind.”
- “One breath for balance, the next for the strike.”
- “Form before fury — that was the academy way.”

---

### **6. Seeds of Shattering**

_Moments that foreshadow his loss of confidence later in Act I._

- “That wasn’t how the instructors said it would feel.”
- “The line was bending. It wasn’t supposed to bend.”
- “His hands were slick, not from sweat.”
- “The shouting drowned out the orders.”
- “The enemy didn’t break. Why weren’t they breaking?”
## Checklist

✅ Stay in **third-person limited** — no details Marcus doesn’t personally notice.  
✅ Let **military training** influence what he sees first (formations, weapons, terrain).  
✅ Show emotion through **action, observation, and sensory detail**, not labels.  
✅ Adjust **fire imagery meaning** as the acts progress.  
✅ Internal thoughts should be **short and raw**, not long speeches.
