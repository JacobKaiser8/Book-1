The blood came later. First came the wind — high and dry, whispering through the rocky pass like a thing with teeth. It tugged at the folds of Marcus Veridion’s shirt and carried with it the sharp scent of pine and sweet tang of grape. Below, the vineyards lay in perfect rows, green against red earth, unaware of how close death had come.

The patrol was designed to be routine — a final exercise before the academy gates closed behind him for good. Soon, he’d march in with a real Legion: a hundred Legionarii, shoulder to shoulder. A block of the unrelenting might of the Imperium Fervelii Pactum. For now he would march in this training group half the size.

As his company marched forward, flanked by oppressive gaze of the late summer sun, Marcus mentally moved through his forms. ==Every spare moment is a time to practice== Slice. Step in. Drive. Push with the rest. As one. He was ready. He moved like a blade in training — tall and lean, built more for reach than brute strength. His dark, curly hair clung damp to his forehead, and his mother's green eyes held a sharpness that hadn’t yet been tempered by conflict. 

Still moving through his sequence, Marcus let his mind drift. Losing focus on the real world around him. Imagining what real combat would be like. He would soon be able to win his own glory in battle. The company came to an abrupt halt and Marcus shouldered into the student in front of him. Braccus sharply turned around and shoved him back. Marcus flailed backward into the hard packed road his hand skidded against the gravel and scraping his palms raw. The stinging sensation shot through his body as he winced in pain. 

"Watch where you're going!" snapped Braccus. 

The other boy was the son of a Centurion — a veteran’s son, bred on discipline and scars. Marcus's father was the imperator of the south. He did not live in the same forge of battle of the front lines.  That hard and often physical discipline showed in the larger boy. 

Marcus picked himself up off the ground, the dirt and grit grinding further into his scalped hand as he pushed off the road. Standing at full height, he stood taller than Braccus, glaring down at him. He tightened his hand on the hilt of his sword, the pain from his fresh wound searing into his palm, boiling his emotions further.

His anger began to muddle his thoughts — only focusing on the combatant in front of him. ==What does this oaf think he is doing== Marcus made a step forward letting his hate bubble to the surface. The blood in his ears pounded amplified by the growing quiet around him. The circle of on lookers started to form. He saw Braccus's muscles flex and coil as he prepared himself to go to arms. The air grew thick with anticipation as everyone ready themselves for what was to happen next.

"By the Emperor's grace! What are you damn idiots doing!"

The sharp command from the Optio cut the tension like cold steel. The two boys quickly snapped to attention looking straight ahead. The Optio was from the 91st Legion of the south ==description of the Optio== and tasked to lead academy boys on their last practice patrol of the year. He was in no mood to deal with two youth who knew nothing of the horror of war snap at each other like two wolf pups. 

"Are you suffering from heatstroke or are you simply the dumbest two citizens in all the Imperium? You really think that your pithy, childish, concerns are more than that of the greater whole? You "feel" angry? You "feel" hurt?" he stretched out each use of feel, mocking the two students and instantly making them realize the absurdness of there fight. 

"Do you believe that you can abandon your duties of the column without it dragging the rest of us down with you? You think you are ready to face the permanent consequences of your actions? Are you prepared to know the _real_ pain of consequence?!" The Optio's voice grew with every question until he was bellowing in their faces, flecks of spit spraying across their faces. 

The Optio drew his gladius. It gleamed with oil in the sunlight. He pointed it at each boy in turn. "If you think that you are ready for war, let me see it. Draw your blade. Show us all that you are _somehow_ more ready than the rest!"

Marcus's heart was thundering now. The adrenaline from almost coming to blows now thoroughly pumping through his body. He stood stock still, frozen in fear. His muscles tensed hard enough to strain. His thighs began to burn clenching in stress. His anxiety grew overwhelmingly in his chest. His mind raced to find a solution, a way out. What was he suppose to do? He couldn't fight a seasoned Optio! He would cut through instantly! At the very least, he would be wounded, cut clean open forced to recover for months in the infirmary. Then what would his father think. He would be banished from the military. His breathing started intensified as he saw his life begin to crumble; spiraling away from everything he had worked for.

The Optio lowered his sword but his hard expression remained affixed to his brow. "If you think that your problems are greater than those around you, greater than your brothers in the same heat of Fervelii, than I don't need you in my company." Still speaking to the delinquents, he turned and strode towards the front of the column. "Think next time - or you won't get one"

Braccus turned to Marcus and shoved him one last time. The Optio was more than twenty paces away but immediately turned about. He walked straight to Braccus and struck him closed fist square in the face. Braccus crumpled to the floor stunned. "You will walk at the back. Your father will learn of what has transpired today and I will let _him_ handle the rest." Before walking away, he turned back to one of the observing boys, "Untie the mule and hitch this sack of shit to the cart. Let him know what it _really_ means to be a beast of burden."

Marcus couldn't help but allow some satisfaction into his face - the brutal justice of the moment siding in his  favor. Braccus, humiliated and broken,  stood and saluted the officer. ==He shuffled towards the back of the column. "Everyone face front"==

As he walked away, one of the scouts came panting up to the Optio. "Sir, there are the first signs of a fire in the village below. It appears to be more smoke than the from cooking stoves." 

The Optio positioned himself toward the rest of the formation and in a commanding voice he boomed over the growing whispers. “Perfect timing. Maybe you can show the citizens you’re not just here to scuff boots and brawl like drunk conscripts. I'll organize you all into duties once we can assess the situation up close. If this fire gets out of hand we will be here into the night. Lets MOVE!" The last word he dragged out as the students jostled into formation.

Marcus rubbed his palms with his thumb as he tried to remove the small sharp stones embedded in his flesh as all the boys together lurched forward. The erratic moment from before already just a memory for most of them. Although Marcus appeared as the victor, he carried a new weight of shame. He studied for over a decade only to come inches from throwing it all away. 

The sun had finally dipped beyond the horizon. The cool air biting at the sticky sweat of the boys as they hustled toward the village. It was a relief from the oppressive heat of the summer air and invigorated them to pick up their pace. The thrill of action once again started crept into the them. Although not combat, it was something to do. A welcome break from the monotony of drudging through the verdant hills. 

As they all crested the hill, they could see just beyond the vineyard estates with their shingled roofs. The fire had grown enough in the dusk that they could see the glow alight above the roofs, a bloom of orange in the darkened sky. The wind seemed to accelerate and the night air cut just a little sharper. Optio roared from the front, "Pick up the pace! Let's go!" and the column broke into a uniform jog.

As they arrived at the low walls of the outskirts, they could hear screaming. A shrill scream pierced the air, freezing the blood in their veins. A cold hand gripped Marcus's heart as they passed the threshold and into the village.

There wasn't just one fire but many of the homes had already been a blaze. Chaos flowed in the streets. People were fleeing in every way out of the gates. Marcus could see the fires on the other side of the village like individual pyres, but he couldn't feel the heat yet. Was the night so cold that it could sweep away the heat? 

The Optio turned to them all and started barking orders. 

"Rows 1 and 2, move to organize the people beyond the low wall. Get them out of our way and to a point of safety."

"Rows 3 and 4, unload cart except for the barrels. Empty their wine. Rows 5, I want you on that well and row 6 on that one." He pointed to the two wells that flanked the two closest villas. Each one large enough to support household of family and servants."

"Row 7 though 10, with me. We will do the same on the other side. We will attack this from both sides. We need to extinguish this before it spreads out of control! Move move move!"

They all quickly raced to their duties. Marcus, standing in row 7 moved with purpose up to the front. He watched as the others raced to direct the people, arms outstretched to funnel them the way the company of soldiers had just come. Those from row 5 and 6 already pulling up buckets of water ready to fill the barrels once laden with wine. Marcus felt a twinge of anger as he saw Braccus, bloody faced pull up into the position he vacated previously. 

He didn't have time to dwell further as the Optio started jogging forward. Marcus and the rest followed suit moving straight towards the thick of the blaze. They passed homes and stalls that were just beginning to catch with cinders. A gale of wind sprayed burning flakes from an adjacent buildings over the thatched roof of another. Immediately the hungry flames engulfed the dry tinder and Marcus finally felt the heat of the fire. It pressed like a mask of heat over his face. He drew his arm over his eyes, protecting them from the searing gust that washed over him.

As they continued running, Marcus noted that the people on this side of must have started to clear out first. There passed only a few people at this point. As they turned a corner to navigate around a house blocking their immediate path, a single group of four stragglers brushed passed them; 3 women and a boy who struggling to keep up. They frantically moved passed barely taking notice of the boys in armor. As they hurried passed Marcus, he looked at their faces; their eyes were open wide in terror, the white stark against their grief stricken faces. Their mouth agape yet silent as though the inferno consumed their screams entirely. They looked less like survivors, more like ghosts fleeing the blaze.

Marcus could hardly tear himself away from the haunting scene of the women running passed. This was just a village fire. They would be left with little and would have to work to recover but they were as though their soul itself had perished. Now that he reflected on it, where were all the men? Why weren't they already fighting the fire?

As they came to the other side of the house and upon the villa's courtyard, they arrived on the answer. There were dead littered around the entire scene. Most looked as though they died running in from the outer lying vineyards. A couple were strewn across the stairs of the villas, blood cascading red down the steps.
