Hot, dry summer afternoon wind came rolled over the hills of Ashvara to accost the band of belabored trainees. Marcus Veridion and the rest of the cadets were sore from another day of marching across through the verdant countryside of the Imperium. they were only three days away from Monteverra and already they wanted to turn back. This was supposed to be their final test, their "right of advancement". Marcus imagined "final" was alluding to the many blisters and sore backs that would break from the weight of their field gear. five more days at this pace? He didn't think he could take even one more step.

The *mission* was designed as one last journey, one final triumphant task — march from Monteverra  to Pactora. The start of their journey the home they had trained the last six years and the destination the marble-white crown of the Imperium. At the end of their trial, they would be granted formal induction into the military. They would be given their chance to obtain fight for Ashvara and to win glory for the Emperor. Currently, that gleaming city and the glory of becoming a great Legionarii was leagues away. Only the beating sun and the pain from their overworked muscles occupied their minds now.

The summer days of his Ashvara were generally pleasant. The warming bay  created a salty breeze over the vineyards mixing with the scent from the grapes on the vine into a delicious salty sweet aroma. The lush hillsides were beautiful to behold and the dusty roads provided the avenue for enjoying the tranquil setting. Today however, the salt in the air stung his eyes, the scent of the grapes sickly sour and even the verdant hills appeared muted to the troop. Marcus thought of the comforts of home and how he would give all his father's coin to not be a recruit. He wouldn't mind being a house worker at his in the estate, maybe even a vendor by the docks. Smelling the fishy oil of unsold merchandise day would be better than this torture.

Marcus's mind continued to wander with the possibilities of a lesser responsibility. What would have happened if instead of following in his father's footsteps, he decided to leave it all behind. Would it really have been so terrible? Not obtain glory at the steps of the Emperor's throne? ==More thoughts on how each day might be easier than right now== He was told that all this training, that the many years at the academy would all be worth it once he experienced the exhilaration of battle! The adrenaline! The thrill! Over the many years at the academy, the these now men had fought through mock battles against the other Contubernia, eight on eight battles whose purpose was to prepare them for the real thing. Marcus's father often expressed that did nothing to ready recruits for the anxiety of death and loss that would loomed during a _real_ engagement. The Academy Legatii would counter with fears of putting anything sharper than a wooden sword in the hands of untrained initiates. Fewer letters home to parents about how a boy could not continue due to a loss of an eye or a limb.

"Pick it up men! We are going to be the first Contubernium to make it to the capital this year! Varro and those dogs from The Red Mantles aren't going to beat us again this year!" The booming command came from the front of the column. Cassian Marcell led there group of eight. Marcus always considered him a strong strict man. He had led this group of now Senior Cadets since they were young initiates in the academy. ==He had grown up much like Marcus, son of a Legatus Legionis, and had developed into the quintessential Optio MAKE SURE TO DESCRIBE CASSIAN AS AN ADULT FIGURE AND NOT ANOTHER TRAINEE==, tall, well muscled, and a real work horse. Although he was tough on those under his leadership, Marcus had to admit he was never abusive like some of the other Optio. That didn't prevent him from carrying out swift discipline where required. Many times Marcus found himself cleaning out the war horse stables, washing the lavatories of the academy, or even just standing at attention while the other cadets trained around him. Marcus didn't hate Cassian but his father was the one of the four Imperators of the great Imperium; the great Mountain of the West. As his son, Marcus believed he would one day command men like Cassian. It frustrated him that someone that would eventually answer to him, would force him to do menial and repetitive tasks during training. He wasn't teaching them the interesting strategies of the battlefield, nor was he teaching them about the exciting history of their empire, their climb to greatness. No. Cassian was assigned to be a drill instructor. A burden. He was the one that at the beginning and end of every day, yelled from his position in the yard to slice and chop. "Marcus turn hips as you swing." "Marcus keep your shield up." "Marcus don't take your eyes of your target." None of the moves that Cassiun taught them were flashy or interesting. One month in their fifth year a Legionarius of the special century came and taught them all manner of advanced moves to take down an enemy combatant. They showed them complex spinning motions, precise lunges, and even one maneuver where you could twist the sword out of your opponent's hand while breaking their knee in at the same time. The very next day Cassian had them back to the same boring fundamental techniques. After six years of basic training, Marcus was tired of the monotony.

Marcus dashed his foot against a stone on the path he had stopped paying attention to and was quickly brought back to his current situation. Cassian had  increased the speed of the column and Marcus could feel sweat beading up anew soaking through his tunic. It suctioned to his skin trapping the heat like an oppressive cage against him. 

"MARCUS! By the grace of the Emperor pick up the pace! If I have to march behind you and kick you in the ass to keep up, I'll make you regret wanting to sit down the next time we make camp! You Clod!"

Marcus tightened up the straps of his gear and did as he was told. Whether or not he had any love for Cassian, he wasn't going to let his comrades down. They met as total strangers as initiates, but had grown to be as brothers in arms. Together they were one mission away from overcoming crucible of the Imperium's military academy destined to emerge as hardened weapons for that same Imperium. They had suffered together and bonded through that suffering. Marcus respected and admired all of those men now moving forward ahead with him. They each brought different skills and qualities that made up what he felt like was the perfect team. 

At their lead was their Primus, Kaeso. ==The team chose him?== Marcus saw in him a lot of the same attributes that he saw in his own father. He respected those that he led, he worked with them and would offer advice and encouragement even when he himself was being overwhelmed by the burden given to him. Once during a drill Kaeso demonstrated to Marcus how he could adjust his stance to push through with his back foot and deliver more power with his thrusting jab. Later that day from Lucian that Kaeso had rolled that same foot the day before trying to perform a jumping spear thrust. Kaeso had demonstrated the adjust Marcus needed without so much as a wince of pain.
