Hot, dry summer afternoon wind came rolled over the hills of Ashvara to accost the band of belabored trainees. Marcus Veridion and the other recruits had been marching across the countryside of the Imperium for three long days. They were told it was their final test, their "right of advancement". Marcus imagined "final" was alluding to the many blisters, sore backs, and tired knees that would break each one of them before they could accomplish their so-called mission.

The *mission* was designed as one last journey, one final triumphant task — March from Monteverra where they had trained the last six years, to Pactora, the marble-white crown of the Imperium. At the end of their trial, they would be granted formal induction into the military. They would be given their chance to obtain glory for Ashvara and to fight for the Emperor. Right now however, that gleaming city and the glory of becoming one of the great Legionarii seemed leagues away. Only the beating sun and the weight of their equipment occupied their minds now.

Typically the summer days of his home were pleasant. The warming bay would create a salty breeze over the vineyards of the countryside that would mix with the scent from the grapes on the vine into a delicious salty sweet aroma. The verdant hillsides were beautiful to behold and the dusty roads provided the avenue for enjoying the picture perfect setting. However, during this laborious excursion, the salt in the air stung his eyes, the scent of the grapes smelled spoiled and even the verdant hills appeared more muted to him. He would give any amount of coin right now to not be a recruit. He wouldn't mind being a house worker at his father's estate, maybe even a vendor by the docks. The oily sour smell of fish all day would be better than anything he had felt in the past few days.

Marcus's mind wandered with the possibilities of being a regular citizen of the Imperium. What would have happened if instead of following in his father's footsteps, he had decided to leave it all behind. Would it really have been so terrible to not obtain glory at the steps of the Emperor's throne? He was told that all this training, that the many years at the academy would all be worth it once he experienced the exhilaration of battle. Throughout their years at the academy, they had experienced mock battles against the other Contubernia, eight on eight battles whose purpose was to prepare them for the real thing. Marcus's father felt that that did nothing to ready them for the anxiety of death that would loom over them during their first _real_ engagement. The Academy Legatii felt putting anything sharper than a wooden sword in the hands of untrained initiates would result in fewer graduations. 

"Pick it up men! We are going to be the first Contubernium to make it to the capital this year! Varro and those dogs from The Red Mantles aren't going to beat us again this year!" came the booming command from Cassian Marcell at the front of their column. Cassian Marcell was a strong strict man. He had led this group of eight since they were young initiates, first years, in the academy. ==He had grown up much like Marcus, son of a Legatus Legionis, and had developed into the quintessential Optio MAKE SURE TO DESCRIBE CASSIAN AS AN ADULT FIGURE AND NOT ANOTHER TRAINEE==, tall, well muscled, and a real work horse. Although he was tough on those in his stewardship, he was never abusive like some of the other Optio. That didn't prevent him from carrying out swift discipline where required. Many times Marcus found himself cleaning out the war horse stables, washing the corridors of the academy, or even just standing at attention while the other cadets trained around him. Marcus didn't hate Cassian but his father was the one of the four Imperators of the great Imperium; the Mountain of the West. As his son, Marcus knew he would one day command men like Cassian. It frustrated him that someone he would eventually command, was forcing him to do what seemed like menial repetitive tasks. He wasn't teaching them the interesting strategies of the battlefield, nor was he teaching them about the war heavy history of their empire. Cassian was assigned to these eight cadets the day they entered the academy to be their drill instructor. He was the one that at the beginning and end of every day, drilled into them horizontal slices and overhead chops. How to lock shields with the man next to you and how to jab with the spear. These weren't the flashy moves they had learned in their fifth year from the Legionarius of the special Century. Those warriors showed them spins, lunges, and even one maneuver where you could twist the sword out of your opponent's hand while breaking their knee in at the same time. No, Cassian was the man that made them practice what he called their "fundamentals". After six years Marcus was able to see why they were so important but couldn't bring himself to change the way he felt towards the man who taught them to him.

Cassian increased his speed at the front of the column and Marcus felt the sweat bead up anew and soak through his tunic. It suctioned to his skin trapping the heat like a cage against him. 

"MARCUS! By the grace of the Emperor pick up the pace! If I have to march behind you and kick you in the ass to keep up, I'll make you regret wanting to sit down the next time we make camp!"

Marcus tightened up his gear and picked up the pace. Whether or not he had any love for Cassian, he wasn't going to let his comrades down. They met as total strangers as initiates, now brothers in arms as senior recruits. They had gone through the crucible of the academy and were almost out as hardened weapons. They had suffered together and bonded through that suffering. Marcus respected and admired each of them. They all had different skills and qualities that made up what he felt like was the perfect team. 

At their lead was their Primus, Kaeso. Marcus saw in him a lot of the same attributes that he saw in his own father. he respected those that he led, he worked with them and would offer advice and encouragement even when he himself was being overwhelmed by the burden given to him. Once during a drill Kaeso demonstrated to Marcus how he could adjust his stance to push through with his back foot and deliver more power with his thrusting jab. Later that day from Lucan that Kaeso had rolled that same foot the day before trying to perform a jumping spear thrust. Kaeso had demonstrated the adjust Marcus needed without so much as a wince of pain.
